h1 :: Anyone can send emails to your customers pretending to be you
p class="lead" :: Your domain publishes no DMARC record, and its SPF policy ends in a soft fail. A stranger can therefore send email to your customers that appears to come from your reservations address: a deposit request, a table confirmation, a link. Nothing alerts you when it happens. The second finding, a menu page that never loads, is easier to see but easier to fix. Both can be resolved today.
h2 :: Your domain has no instruction to reject a forged sender
p class="lede" :: Two DNS records determine whether a message from your domain is authentic: SPF and DMARC. The first is published but permissive; the second is absent. Neither prevents forgery on its own. Together they amount to an invitation.
p class="kicker" :: Highest impact · affects every customer
p style="color:var(--ink-2);font-size:16p :: An email arrives from reservations@aftereightsousse.tn. It reaches the inbox, survives a first glance, and asks a customer to confirm a booking by paying a deposit into an account that is not yours. Without DMARC, the receiving server has no instruction to reject or quarantine it, and no report reaches you afterwards.
p class="lede" style="margin-top:var(--s5 :: A single-line DMARC record in monitor mode costs nothing and cannot break your mail, because at p=none it instructs no server to block anything. It simply starts sending you reports. Move to p=quarantine once those reports come back clean, typically after two to four weeks.

h2 :: Two doors onto your domain are standing open
p class="lede" :: This finding looks the most alarming and is the least urgent of the three. It matters for one specific reason: the address would be real, and the certificate would be valid.
li :: Your DNS still points two names at GitHub. mail. and ftp.aftereightsousse.tn both resolve to 185.199.108.153, a GitHub Pages address. Nothing of yours is hosted there.
li :: GitHub answers with its own placeholder. Request either name and you get Site not found · GitHub Pages, which proves GitHub is listening and that no repository claims the name.
li :: The ownership check was never performed. GitHub refuses to host a stranger's page on your domain only if you have published a _github-pages-challenge- TXT record proving the domain is yours. No such record exists in your zone.
li :: A stranger can therefore claim it without touching anything of yours. They point their own repository at mail.aftereightsousse.tn; GitHub sees that DNS already points at it and begins serving. Your DNS is never modified. The page appears within minutes, on your domain, behind a CN=*.github.io certificate that browsers accept.
p style="margin:0" :: What this is worth, and what it is not. No password and no customer data are exposed, because the site has no login and sets no cookies. What is at stake is your name: a link people already half-trust, announcing that the menu has moved and asking for a card number. That is why it ranks below the dead menu. It only works if somebody notices you, and nobody has to notice a menu that will not load.
p class="lede" style="margin-top:var(--s5 :: The fix is to delete both DNS records, since nothing lives at either address. If they are needed later, re-add them together with the verification TXT record, which protects every subdomain at once.
p class="lede" style="margin-top:var(--s5 :: None of these hands anyone your data. The source map is notable in the other direction: it is how I read every file on this page without touching your server. That is a fair trade for a brochure site and a poor habit for anything with a login behind it. If you leave it published, be aware that the backend URL and the full routing logic are public.
p style="margin:0" :: The bare domain, for completeness. https://aftereightsousse.tn fails its certificate check, because only www has a matching one. Visiting http:// works: it redirects to https://www. and the site loads. So this affects only the visitor who types the bare domain and meets an https attempt first. Some will see a warning and leave.
